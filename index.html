Computational neuroscience of memory

We study how long-term information is encoded in the brain, with a focus on associative spatial memory. We combine high-throughput behaviour, large-scale neural recordings in freely moving mice, and machine learning.

Pablo Jercog Team · Instituto Cajal, CSIC · Madrid

Our goal

Memory, from the hippocampus to the medial temporal lobe, studied with behaviour, recordings and models.

Our research is dedicated to uncovering how long-term information is encoded in the brain, with a focus on associative spatial memory. Understanding the neural mechanisms underlying memory is essential not only for advancing neuroscience but also for tackling major societal challenges.

Our work directly contributes to strategies for diagnosing and treating brain disorders by revealing how memory-related structures, such as the hippocampus and medial temporal lobe, function in both healthy and diseased states. Furthermore, by modeling neuronal representations and cognitive processes, our research fuels the development of next-generation artificial intelligence, providing biologically inspired algorithms for learning, adaptation, and decision-making.

Leveraging advanced methodologies, including large-scale electrophysiological recordings, high-throughput behavioral assays, and computational modeling, we generate essential knowledge at the intersection of neuroscience, medicine, and AI, advancing both brain health and technological innovation.

1

High-throughput behavioral tasks

Unsupervised, fully computer-controlled tasks.

  • Fully computer controlled freely moving behavioral task to measure spatial memory recall, during neural activity recordings.
  • Parametric, high-number-of-trials test to measure learning and recall of spatial memories.
  • Memory accuracy measured over many trials per session (~20), for many daily sessions per animal (~200).

For more details see our current publication.

2

Neuronal recordings in freely moving mice

We perform tetrode-based electrophysiological recordings and miniaturized 1-photon calcium-imaging recordings of large neuronal populations in freely moving mice.

See more details in our published electrophysiology and calcium imaging experiments.

3

Neuronal population data analysis

Machine learning to relate population activity to behaviour.

  • We use neuronal decoders to measure the amount of information encoded in the activity of the cells, and to track learning over time at the population level (reference).
  • We use information theory in high-dimensional neuron population vector spaces to test which sensory input features better explain neuronal responses, to understand how information is encoded (reference).

Projects

Four current lines of work, from abstract rules in the hippocampus to language models for drug discovery.

Neural representation of abstract rules in the hippocampus

Calcium imagingHidden Markov ModelsMedial temporal lobe

Understanding how the hippocampus encodes abstract concepts such as rules is crucial for deciphering cognitive flexibility. In this project, we combine high-throughput behavioral tasks with calcium imaging of medial temporal lobe areas to investigate how rule-based memory guides complex task resolution. By applying Hidden Markov Models to segment behavioral data over time, we correlate neural activity patterns with distinct rule implementations, unveiling the neural dynamics underlying abstract rule representation.

Disentangling memory and actions from behavior using POMDPs

POMDPs8-port mazeModeling

To understand how animals balance memory and decision policies in dynamic environments, we model behavior in our 8-port maze task using Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs). By simulating agents under different environmental conditions where rule volatility changes in real time, we aim to disentangle the contribution of memory, expressed in the a priori internal beliefs, from policy adaptation during task resolution. Our approach integrates mice behavioral data fitting with computational modeling to reveal how animals update strategies in response to changing uncertainty.

Large language models for digital twins in drug discovery

LLMsDigital twinsPrecision medicine

This project leverages large-scale data banks to develop large language models (LLMs) for creating digital twins in drug discovery and testing. By integrating vast biological and pharmacological datasets, our models aim to simulate drug interactions, predict therapeutic outcomes, and optimize experimental designs. This approach enhances precision medicine by enabling in silico testing, accelerating hypothesis generation, and reducing reliance on traditional trial-and-error methodologies.

Exploring the dimensionality of spatial representations in the dorsal hippocampus

ElectrophysiologyPlace cellsNavigation

Using electrophysiological recordings from freely moving mice, we investigate the high-dimensional structure of neuronal representations in the dorsal hippocampus. Building on our discovery that place-cell responses are modulated by heading direction relative to reference points, we aim to identify additional behavioral variables that shape neuronal activity. By analyzing how these representations evolve as animals learn to navigate novel environments, we seek to understand how preexisting network structures support spatial cognition and adaptive behavior.

FilledPh.D. position: neural representations of spatial memory in freely moving mice (2019)

Project: Neuronal representations of spatial memories: immune ablation of synaptic mechanisms of memory formation as an animal model of memory malfunction.

Keywords: spatial memory, neural activity, hippocampus and cortex interaction, NMDA receptors, calcium imaging, animal behavior, quantitative data analysis.

This is a unique opportunity for a strongly motivated candidate interested in studying how information is stored long-term in the brain. The candidate will develop a set of read-outs to measure how memories are generated, retained and recalled using calcium imaging data and animal behavioral readouts. The behavior is combined with a cutting-edge freely moving calcium imaging technology (nVista miniscopes) to simultaneously monitor the activity of large numbers of individual neurons over many days. Using our recently established reversible antibody-mediated disruption of NMDA receptor function in mice (Planagumà et al. 2015), we will characterize, at the behavioral and neural circuit levels, the mechanisms of memory formation and recall in normal and malfunctioning brains. The candidate will perform quantitative analyses of large data sets using modern statistical analysis and machine learning.

The project was developed within a collaboration between several investigators at IDIBAPS (Barcelona): Josep Dalmau, MD, PhD, Jaime de la Rocha PhD, Albert Compte PhD and Pablo Jercog PhD. It was supported by the CELLEX foundation and developed at IDIBAPS, with enrolment in the Biomedicine PhD program of the University of Barcelona.

FilledAdvanced technician: synaptopathy in animal models of autoimmune encephalitis (17 April 2019)

We sought an advanced-level technician to study the behavior and neuronal activity of mice during a spatial navigation task to test memory and amnesia: a one-year contract, with the possibility of applying for fellowships to start a PhD in our team.

The project, funded by the Cellex Foundation under the Experimental Neuroimmunology Program led by Dr. Josep Dalmau, aims to determine how auto-antibodies from patients with a synaptopathy (for example, anti-NMDAR encephalitis) alter memory and behavior in validated mouse models of the pathologies, in parallel with investigations in patients with these diseases. The candidate would be exposed to complex behavioral tasks and electrophysiological and calcium imaging recordings in freely behaving mice.
